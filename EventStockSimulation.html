Stock Market Simulation

What would you buy?

Ever wondered if you have what it takes to beat the market? In the Stock Market Simulation, you'll step into the shoes of a real portfolio manager — analysing trends, weighing risk, and building a portfolio designed to meet your client's goals. From September 28th to October 16th you'll research, strategize, and make your calls before bringing it all to XAVAGE day, where you'll present your portfolio and defend the thinking behind every decision. This isn't about guessing lucky stocks — it's about sharp research, smart risk management, and a strategy that can hold up under pressure. Are your instincts investor-grade?

How it works

Round 1 is set. Round 2 is coming soon.

Round 1 — Trading

28 Sept – 16 Oct, online
Advancing: top 10

Each team gets a client case study and a virtual starting portfolio of $250,000. The case outlines the client's financial situation, values, investment goals, risk tolerance and constraints. Acting as the client's investment advisors, teams must analyse the client, research the available market (publicly traded securities on the NASDAQ or NYSE — no options trading), construct a $250,000 portfolio aligned with the client's objectives, and develop an investment thesis covering expected returns, risk, diversification and valuation.

Teams submit their final portfolio and rationale by October 16th. The HODs review submissions from the 16th to the 20th, and the top 10 finalists are announced on 20th October.

Round 2 — Coming soon

Details closer to 28th October

The top 10 finalists, announced 20th October, bring it all to a live pitch in front of a panel of judges. The full format for the final round drops closer to 28th October.

Rules

What you can argue about on the day.

Eligibility & team composition

Open to Grades 11 and 12. Individuals are permitted; a team has a maximum size of 3. Schools can send multiple teams, but they may not communicate with each other. Up to one substitution is allowed during Round 1, but not after a team has qualified for Round 2.

Conduct & disqualification

Teams are expected to maintain professional, respectful conduct throughout. Minor first-time issues — inappropriate communication, failing to follow presentation procedure — get a warning. Repeated or more serious violations can cost points or other penalties. Cheating, plagiarism, falsifying information, unauthorized outside assistance, rule manipulation or harassment are grounds for disqualification. The organising committee and judging panel have final authority, and their decisions are final.

Tiebreakers

After Round 1: higher portfolio return, then higher risk-adjusted return, then higher diversification/construction score, then higher rationale score — still tied, the judging panel decides on overall submission quality.

Tiebreakers for Round 2 will be announced with the rest of the format.

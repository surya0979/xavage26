The Courting

Real cases, assigned sides, one verdict.

The Courting is XAVAGE's courtroom. Eight teams, four real corporate cases (the kind sitting in actual courtrooms right now) and a side you don't get to choose. Your first brief lands 24 hours before the event; you arrive ready to argue it through openings, arguments and closings while judges score every exchange. Win, and the cases start arriving on the spot, with 20 minutes of pen-and-paper prep before you're back on your feet. The last two teams argue a fresh case before a jury. If you're quick in an argument and quicker in a comeback, this room was built for you.

How it works

Round 1 is set. Rounds 2 and 3 are coming soon.

Round 1 — The Prepared Case

2h 45m, live moot
Entering: 8 teamsAdvancing: 4

Four corporate case briefs, four matches. Each brief is released to its two assigned teams exactly 24 hours before the event, along with the side they must argue — prosecution or defence, no swapping. Matches run one after another in front of the same bench.

Each team gets 15 minutes, split 5-5-5: opening statement, arguments, closing statement, alternating with the opposing team so every claim can be answered. Judges may ask short questions at the end of a segment and score both teams. The higher-scoring team from each match advances; teams not arguing sit in the gallery.

Rules

What you can argue about on the day.

Eligibility & team composition

Open to Grades 9–12. Teams of 3 or 4, all from the same school. The cap is 8 teams, first come first served — one team per school until a week before the event, after which unfilled slots open to second teams. Substitutions are allowed any time before Round 1 begins; after that, none. A team that loses a member mid-event continues with 3, and a team below 3 forfeits.

What you may and may not bring

Allowed in Round 1: printed research, annotated copies of the brief, handwritten notes at the counsel table. Allowed in every round: pen, paper, water.

Not allowed during any live round: laptops, tablets, phones, smartwatches, earphones, or internet access in any form. Rules for Rounds 2 and 3 will be announced with the rest of the format.

Timing

Round 1 presentations are 15 minutes per team (5 opening, 5 arguments, 5 closing), with a 10-minute settle-in before the round starts. Timing for Rounds 2 and 3 will be announced once the format is revealed.

Conduct & disqualification

Warning: interrupting opposing counsel out of turn, addressing the other team instead of the bench, or misstating what the brief says. After a warning, each repeat costs 5% of the round score. Claimed "facts" that appear nowhere in the brief are struck and penalised at the judges' discretion.

Immediate removal: phone or internet use during a live round or prep window, or abusive language toward any participant, judge or volunteer. Disqualification is called jointly by the two event heads after consulting the bench, never by one person alone.

Tiebreakers

Round 1: level totals go to the higher argumentation-and-rebuttal score, then the higher legal-knowledge score, then a 2-minute sudden-death rebuttal by one speaker per team, decided by judges' vote. Tiebreakers for Rounds 2 and 3 will be announced with the rest of the format.

Sides are assigned, never chosen, so preparing for Round 1 means preparing to win the side you're given. All case briefs come from real, recent or ongoing corporate disputes and are vetted by the event heads in advance. Arguments must be built from the brief and general legal principle — outside "evidence" a team happens to know is inadmissible.

Judging

Scored out of 10 by every judge on the bench.

Argumentation & rebuttal

Clear, structured arguments that answer the other side directly — real rebuttals, not repeated openings.

35%
Legal knowledge & use of the brief

Accurate legal terminology, correct reading of the facts, arguments anchored in the brief.

30%
Presentation & courtroom manner

Clear delivery, persuasive without theatrics, proper address to the bench, composure under questioning.

20%
Team coordination

Sensible division of speaking roles, no contradictions between speakers, smooth handovers.

15%

Judged by 2–3 people with a legal background per bench — a law or global politics teacher, a practising lawyer from the school's network, or a law-student alumnus. How the final is judged will be announced with the rest of the Round 2 and 3 format.
